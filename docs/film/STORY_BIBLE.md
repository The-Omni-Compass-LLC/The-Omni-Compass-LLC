## 1. The film on one page

**Title.** YIELD LIMIT LINE.

**Form.** A live-action short film of about sixteen minutes, written to be shot with a crew or made shot by shot with text-to-video and image-to-video tools, and written as the centrepiece of a feature (section 11 carries the whole feature).

**Genre.** A procedural thriller with a mystery inside it: a single night, a storm outside, a system under load, and a question nobody has answered in fourteen months.

**Logline.** On the night a hurricane brushes Florida's Space Coast, the engineer who keeps a hospital's computers alive must find out what has woken her at 3:12 every night for a year, and then decide whether to win the room with a big beautiful number or a small true one.

**Tagline.** One night is a story. Three runs is a receipt.

**The question the film asks.** How far can anything be pushed, a steel part, a hospital's computers, a woman, a claim, before it stops springing back?

**The answer it gives.** Every piece to its own line, all of them at once, and never past it. Nobody holds a system together with their hands. And no number is worth saying without its receipt.

**Why it can be made now.** It needs one family garage, one glass office, one hospital corridor, one CT room, one data hall, one car and one causeway at dawn. Every shot is written so it can be made alone, in any order, by a crew or by a video model (section 14).

## 2. The title

A *yield limit* is the line every piece of steel carries inside it. Load a part up to that line and let go, and it springs back as if nothing happened. Load it one hair past, and it keeps a little of the stretch forever. Past that, it necks and breaks. Ray Arceneaux spent thirty-one years watching that line on a dial for parts that flew off the pad at the Cape.

The film's title carries the line four ways:

- **The steel.** The coupon Ray stretches in the first act, and the one Del stretches, and stops, in the last.
- **The system.** Every muscle of the hospital's computing (the pods, the machines, the cooling, the power) has its own line; the story's night is about loading each to its line and never past.
- **The woman.** Del has been running past her own line for fourteen months. Her father hears it in her voice: "You been ringing past your line a long time."
- **The truth.** A claim pushed past its receipt is a part pushed past its yield. It may still look whole. It will not spring back.

The words come from the company's own telephone line, 1-888-DEX-YLL-1, which The Omni-Compass LLC reads as its engine runs: DEX, the data exchange (the input); YLL, the yield limit line (every muscle to its own limit and never past it); 1, one closed loop (one body, one brain).

## 3. What the film is about

**Every piece to its own line.** The hospital's autoscaler, its cluster, its cooling and its power each do exactly what they were built to do. Alone, and late. Nobody is wrong, and the night still fails, because nobody watches all the pieces at once except a woman with a pager at 3:12 in the morning. The film's answer is not a smarter piece: it is something that sits on top of the pieces and moves them as one body, each to its own limit, never past it.

**One night is a story. Three runs is a receipt.** The rival vendor wins the night with a dollar number it bought by parking the machines the emergency department needed. Del refuses to sell her own night at all. The only result she speaks is a measured one, with its record behind it, and she says what it does not show.

**The babysitting tax is a person.** The cost of a system that needs watching is not a line on a slide. It is Del, upright on her bed in the blue light, waiting for the next page. The film ends when she can sleep, not when the system is perfect: the 4:40 page still comes, and it is real.

**Do no harm.** Cheap at two in the morning is easy; cheap with nobody hurt is the job. Harm is judged at the organism, the patient on the CT table, never at the bill.

## 4. The world: Florida's Space Coast

**Where.** Brevard County, on Florida's Atlantic coast: a long county of barrier islands, lagoon and mainland, held together by causeways. The Indian River Lagoon runs its whole length, shallow and wide, a mirror at dawn. Across it lie Merritt Island and the Cape, where the country has launched rockets since the 1950s. On the mainland, from south to north: Palm Bay, the county's largest city; Melbourne, with its causeways to the beaches; Cocoa and Cocoa Beach; Titusville, the county seat, across the water from the launch pads. US 1 runs along the lagoon; US 192 runs west from the beaches through Melbourne; Interstate 95 runs behind them all.

**Who.** At the 2020 census, 606,612 people lived in the county. Its working life still turns on the launch business, aerospace and defence contractors, the Space Force station, the hospitals, tourism and the water; it is also a county of retirees, of families who came for the space program and stayed, and of newer arrivals from the Caribbean and Latin America. The film's people are drawn from that life: a Haitian-born machinist who worked the launch years and the daughter he raised in Palm Bay; a Puerto Rican charge nurse; an Air Force veteran running a hospital's infrastructure; a retired structures engineer on the hospital's board in Titusville; a CT technologist on nights.

**When.** Late October, at the end of hurricane season (it runs from 1 June to 30 November). A storm, Hurricane Lucinda (fictional), stays offshore but drags its bands across the coast after dark: tropical-storm-force wind, sheets of rain, the power grid failing street by street, generators taking over.

**The rule of the place.** Everything here has been built to a line: the shuttle stack, the seawall, the hurricane shutter, the generator's rating. People here know what a margin is.

## 6. The people

**DELPHINE "DEL" ARCENEAUX (34).** Site reliability engineer on call for Cape Shore Regional Medical Center's computing, for fourteen months without a full night's sleep. Born in Palm Bay to a Haitian father who worked the launch years. Quick, dry, proud, and quietly past her own line. *Wants:* the pages to stop. *Needs:* to stop being the only thing that holds the system together, and to let a smaller true thing stand in a room that wants a big one. *Wound:* every miracle product the hospital has bought has been a slide. *Arc:* from holding everything by hand to trusting a body that holds itself; from guarding a number to giving it with its receipt. *Voice:* low, few words, jokes as armour. *Key line:* "One night is a story." *Look (kept the same in every shot):* deep brown skin, close-cropped natural hair, tired eyes, slim; a charcoal sweatshirt, black jeans, a navy hospital lanyard.

**RAYMOND "RAY" ARCENEAUX (69).** Del's father. Born in Haiti, came to Florida young, ran the tensile test bench at a launch contractor's materials laboratory for thirty-one years. A widower who still boards his windows a day early. Teaches by showing. *Wants:* his daughter to sleep. *Needs:* nothing he does not have. *Function:* the film's conscience and its physics teacher; his dial is the film's first gauge and its last. *Voice:* warm, unhurried, Kreyòl in the cadence ("Pitit mwen": my child; "Dòmi, pitit": sleep, child).

**THEO VANCE (46).** Vice President of Infrastructure. Air Force years in the posture, a hospital fleece over a white shirt. Squeezed between a board that wants a dollar number and engineers who keep telling him the truth is smaller. *Wants:* something he can defend at seven in the morning. *Arc:* from "bring me a big one" to "bring me a small true one, and I'll lose with it" to reading the emergency department's log aloud on the call. He is not a villain; he is the reader the film is written for.

**SLOANE MERCER (38).** Enterprise lead for Perihelion (fictional), whose agent does not sit on top of a hospital's controllers: it replaces them. Polished, warm, never rushed, and sincere: she believes her deck. *Function:* the temptation, given a human face. Her slide makes three claims with no receipt; her night "cuts compute cost thirty-one percent" by parking the machines the CT room needed. *Her last scene:* off the record, one honest question ("How do you sleep, doing it your way?"), and the film lets her almost smile.

**NADIA REYES (52).** Night charge nurse in the emergency department. Puerto Rican, from Palm Bay; Spanish for emphasis; knows Del's voice from a hundred calls at 3:12. *Function:* the stakes, spoken plainly ("Four minutes, mija"), and the film's last message ("sleep"). Her tracking board teaches the audience the film's three signs before the wall uses them.

**DION PRICE (26).** CT technologist on nights. Calm, few words. *Function:* the eyes on the spinning wheel.

**GLORIA HAYWOOD (74).** Board member; retired structures engineer, Titusville. Large tortoiseshell glasses, a roof tarp snapping outside her kitchen window. *Function:* the referee. She asks the only question that matters ("Between what hours?"), names the principle ("Cheap with nobody hurt is the job"), and carries the old discipline of the Cape: nothing flies on one test.

**The system (not a person, and never given a face or a voice).** Four muscles, each on its own monitor: pods, machines, cooling, power. Their native controllers (the autoscaler, the cluster) work exactly as built. Perihelion's agent replaces the autoscaler and optimizes the bill. Omni-Compass sits on top of the native controllers, watching first, and on Del's word drives them as one body.

## 7. How Omni-Compass is shown (rules for every department)

The film shows Omni-Compass as it works, and nothing more:

1. **It sits on top of what a system already runs, never in its place.** The autoscaler stays; the cluster stays. ("It didn't sit on top of it. It sat in its chair" is said of the rival, never of Omni-Compass.)
2. **It has modes, and a person chooses them.** Watching, then autopilot. Del throws the switch; the software does not decide to take over.
3. **The kill switch is separate and instant.** Del uses one on the rival's agent; it hands every setting back to native at once.
4. **Every muscle to its own line, never past it.** On the wall each muscle's needle rises to its line and holds. The generator's line is drawn as a hard edge and never crossed.
5. **Power given is not a loss.** Omni-Compass ran more machines than the rival that night; Del calls it what the company calls it: power given. It bought a scan.
6. **It is not magic.** It does not prevent the disk failing at 4:40. That page comes, and Del answers it.
7. **It has no voice, no face, no avatar.** It is seen only through the signs and the needles.
8. **The film claims nothing from its own night.** The one result spoken is the record's, with its receipt (section 15).

The three signs are the company's own: a green circle means working, a yellow diamond means heading for its line, a red octagon (a stop sign, never a red circle) means over its line. Nadia's board teaches them; the wall uses them.

## 8. The structure

The running times are in `SCENES.md` (written from the shot list, so they never drift). The beats:

1. **Opening image.** 3:12. Del upright before the second buzz. A year of red circles on the calendar.
2. **The lesson.** Ray's garage: the needle climbs, hesitates at the yield, springs back; then the snap. "Past that, she don't remember nothing." The idea the whole film turns on is planted as physics.
3. **The setup.** Theo's office: Perihelion's slide; "It replaces it" (planted); "one switch hands everything back" (planted); "the outpatient centers ... will push every scan they've got" (planted); the board at seven.
4. **The stakes.** Nadia: four minutes on a frozen CT viewer; the three signs taught.
5. **The investigation.** The data hall: Del draws the night on the glass in grease pencil. 3:00 the clinics send; 3:05 the autoscaler notices, late; the pods wait; 3:09 a machine; 3:12, her. The suspect (PRHL-AGENT) is cleared: it is a week old, the pages are a year old. The solution: nobody is wrong; nobody watches all the pieces at once.
6. **The midpoint.** The power fails. The generator's line: 70%. A trauma inbound. Perihelion's agent, seeing a budget, parks machines that were about to work. Red octagons.
7. **All is lost.** The CT viewer spins. The kill switch restores native, and native is late.
8. **The choice.** One week of trial, no receipt, a Category 3 outside. Del switches Omni-Compass from watching to autopilot.
9. **The climax.** The needles rise, each to its own line, and hold. Octagons to diamonds to circles. The scan loads.
10. **The honest beat.** 4:40: a disk is failing. "That one's real." She goes to fix it.
11. **The reckoning.** The board on video at dawn. Sloane's thirty-one percent, and the hours it was bought in. The analyst's modelled slide, deleted. Del's one number, with its receipt, and what it does not show. "Three runs is a receipt." Ninety days.
12. **The final image.** The garage. Del loads a fresh coupon to the yield and stops. The needle springs back to zero. She sleeps. Nadia's message: every shape a green circle.

## 9. The mystery

**The question.** What wakes Del at 3:12, night after night?

**The clues, all on screen before the answer.** The calendar's circles drawn at the same hour; "Same time?" "Same time."; Theo's line that the clinics push their scans before they lock up; the grease-pencil timeline; the machine removed at 3:40 and the pods evicted at 3:52, every night.

**The red herring.** PRHL-AGENT, recurring at three o'clock. It looks like the culprit and is cleared by a date: installed last Tuesday, against fourteen months of pages.

**The solution.** There is no culprit. Each controller is right alone and late together; the only thing joining them is a person with a pager. Ray's line ("On a rocket, somebody watches all the pieces at once") is the key, given in the first act and turned in the second.

**The twist.** The agent that did not cause the pages causes the night's real harm: it replaced the autoscaler and parked the machines the CT room was about to need.

**The second mystery: will Del say the big number?** The board wants a dollar figure; an analyst hands her a modelled one. The audience does not know until her thumb deletes it.

## 10. Motifs and rhymes

- **The needle.** Ray's dial (springs back; remembers; snaps), the wall's gauges (rise to the line and hold), Ray's dial again (Del stops at the yield; zero).
- **The coupon.** Broken on the nightstand; broken again in Ray's hands; carried in Del's pocket to the data hall; laid beside a whole one at the end.
- **3:12.** The page, the calendar, the grease pencil, Nadia's greeting, Del's "Me. At three-twelve."
- **The buzz.** The same designed sound every time, until the last one brings a word instead of a page.
- **Hands.** "Not one was held together by a man's hands"; Del's finger on the kill switch; Ray's blanket.
- **The signs.** The board teaches them, the wall speaks them, Nadia's last photograph is all circles.

## 11. The whole movie

The short is the feature's midpoint: the storm night. The feature, about 110 minutes, in eight sequences:

1. **The pages.** Palm Bay, a year of 3:12s. Del's life in fragments: the pager, Ray's garage, Nadia's voice, a hospital buying miracles off slides. Perihelion arrives with a slide and a smile.
2. **The bake-off.** Theo sets two trials. Del installs Omni-Compass to watch, on top of what they run. Gloria joins the board's review and asks for receipts, not demos.
3. **The investigation.** Fourteen months of logs. The clinics, the late autoscaler, the waiting pods. The red herring. Nadia's stroke patient and the four minutes.
4. **The storm night** (the short film): the power cut, the rival's parked machines, the kill switch, the choice, the needles, the scan, the 4:40 disk.
5. **The fallout.** Perihelion's investors push a state procurement built on its slide. Sloane offers Del a job at four times her pay. Ray's heart falters (he, too, ran past his line for years). The first real receipts come in small, and one test reads a LOSS: Del puts it on the page anyway.
6. **The county.** A county commission hearing on a new data center's draw on the grid after a summer of blackouts; the public asks who is accountable for promises made with public power and public money.
7. **Washington.** A Senate subcommittee (fictional) holds a hearing on the power demands of computing and the public money behind them. Sloane testifies with her slide. Del testifies with her receipts, the losses included, and the instructions to check them. Overnight a committee staffer runs the published test harness and reproduces the record; Perihelion's numbers cannot be run.
8. **The launch.** Dawn on the causeway; a rocket climbs off the Cape; Ray watches from a lawn chair in his driveway. In the garage a coupon springs back to zero. Del sleeps through a sunrise for the first time in two years.

The feature's spine is the same as the short's: a system, a woman and a claim, each pushed toward its line; the drama is whether anyone stops at it.

## 12. The look

- **Frame.** Made at 16:9 (1920 × 1080, 24 frames a second); a 2.39:1 letterbox may be added in the edit.
- **Camera grammar.** Still and composed in the data hall (order under strain); handheld only in the emergency department and in the moment of alarm; slow push-ins at every decision; macro inserts for the coupon, the dial and the switch.
- **Lenses.** 24mm for places, 35 to 50mm for people, 85mm for faces at decision, 100mm macro for the needle.
- **Light, by world.** The production file names each: the bedroom's phone-blue, the storm's grey-green, the office's pewter, the hospital's cyan-white, the data hall's blue-white, the generator's amber, the dawn's pink-gold, the garage's morning warmth.
- **Colour key.** The three signs are the film's only saturated colours on the wall: green circle, yellow diamond, red octagon.
- **Design.** Ray's pegboard (every tool outlined, so its absence shows) is the film's picture of a system that knows its own pieces.

## 13. The sound

- **The buzz.** One designed phone buzz used every time it sounds.
- **The ratchet.** The tensile tester's crank; it goes silent before the snap.
- **The diesel.** The generator's thrum felt through the floor after the dark.
- **The hum.** The data hall's fans: the system's breathing.
- **Music.** Sparse; nothing over dialogue that carries a number.
- **Languages.** Ray's Kreyòl ("Pitit mwen", "Dòmi, pitit") and Nadia's Spanish ("mija") are left in the voice; subtitles may carry the Kreyòl.

## 14. Making it with video models

The production file (`production.json`) and its compiled pack (`PROMPTS.json`, `PROMPTS.md`, `REFERENCE_SHEETS.md`, `SHOT_LIST.csv`) are written for any text-to-video or image-to-video tool, and for a human crew.

1. **References first.** Make one image of every character, prop and place from `REFERENCE_SHEETS.md`, choose the best, and keep it. Every shot that names one uses it.
2. **Start frames.** For tools that take an image, make each shot's start frame from its references with its `start_frame` prompt; then the motion from its `prompt`.
3. **One shot, one action.** Each prompt describes what a camera would see: the framing, the angle, the move, the lens, the place, the people as they look, one action, the words, the sound. Nothing is described as a feeling the camera cannot see.
4. **Lengths.** Every shot carries the clip length each tool makes (`generate_seconds`); generate at that length and trim in the edit.
5. **Words.** Tools that make sound take the dialogue in the prompt; for the rest, record the lines (speaker, words, delivery) and lay them in.
6. **No text from a model.** Video models cannot write legible words. Every word the audience reads (a phone, a wall, a slide, a clock) is an overlay in `overlays/`, and every card is in `cards/`.
7. **Continuity.** The same descriptions are repeated, word for word, in every prompt that needs them; the props are their own references.
8. **Review.** A person watches every clip against its shot before it enters the cut.

The tool lengths in the production file are those each tool published when it was written; tools change, and their own pages are read before generating.

## 15. The numbers rule

Every number the audience hears or reads is declared in `production.json`, and `tools/film.py` refuses the film if one is not:

- **The story's own.** Times (3:12, 1:58, 4:40), a road (US 192), a setting of the building (the generator's 70% line), a storm's category.
- **Claims the story refuses.** Perihelion's slide (52% faster, 30% off the bill), its overnight 31%, and the analyst's modelled 44%: shown so that the story can refuse them, and declared as claims with no receipt.
- **Receipts.** The one result spoken, in the reckoning: the pages a person would have been sent across thirteen Kubernetes tests, each run three times, native against Omni-Compass on top, a quarter fewer (down 25.7%), GAIN in all three of our runs, on omni-v21, real software on GitHub's machines. `tools/film.py` reads it again from its record (`results/OVERSIGHT_PAGES.json`, by the pages tool's own reading) every time it runs, and the end card from the website's numbers file (`results/SITE_NUMBERS.json`). If the record changes, the film fails its check until its words do.

Del says what the receipt does not show, too: the bill was not measured, and one night proves nothing.
