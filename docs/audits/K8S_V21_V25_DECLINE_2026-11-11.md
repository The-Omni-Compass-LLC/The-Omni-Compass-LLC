## The answer

The fall is real, and most of it has one cause. Between the two campaigns the governor's decision beat on site changed from
60 s to 10 s (omni-v24, landed 2 November: "the factory beat for its place, 10 s on site, 60 s through a cloud"). Every one of
the 270 Omni arms behind the omni-v21 tables ran at 60 s, and every one of the 270 behind the omni-v25 tables ran at 10 s (each
arm's own `omni_interval.txt`). At 10 s one guard of the conveyance switches on and off every two beats. The guard is omni-v13's:
on a shared host at its top, beside another workload's room, every processor limit given goes back to the operator's, and is
given again only under 70% busy. It does this in the three workloads that run another workload beside the service: KEDA's HTTP
add-on, KEDA on the processor target with the requests, and the fairness test's second application. The serving pods' limit was
raised to 1,850–3,700m, then handed back to the operator's 500m about 20 seconds later, over and over:

- **206 times an arm** in KEDA on the processor and the requests (omni-v21: 3.4)
- **40 times an arm** in KEDA's HTTP add-on (omni-v21: 0.5)
- **52 times an arm** in fairness (omni-v21: 13)

Those three workloads carry **68% of the fall**. The repairs made to the engine after omni-v21 did not cause it (section 3).
The other 32% sits in workloads with no such cycle. In most of them native's own response time also moved between the two
campaigns, so how much of that part is the engine is **not established**.

| omni-v23 (`controller.py`) | a workload's own scale the cluster refuses goes back to native's autoscaler | only in the arm by itself; the nine tables are Omni-Compass on native, where it changes nothing (`docs/OMNI_V23.md`) |

measured on site.

## 4. The mechanism, from the governor's own audit

Each Omni arm, averaged over its 30 arms (three runs, ten repetitions). The counts come from each arm's `audit.jsonl`, and each
processor-limit write is placed by the reason the governor wrote with it. A raise is a share of a machine's idle processor given
to its serving pods ("idle CPU to its N serving pod(s), limit X in place"). A hand-back is a return to the operator's limit
("the shared host at its top ... beside another workload's room, operator's limit 500m in place", or the reset at the end).

| Workload | Beat | Decisions an arm | Writes an arm | Limit raises an arm | Hand-backs to the operator's limit an arm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| KEDA's HTTP add-on | 60 → 10 s | 15 → 90 | 5.2 → 87.6 | 3.7 → 43.0 | 0.5 → 39.7 |
| KEDA on the processor and the requests | 60 → 10 s | 15 → 90 | 33.3 → 452.9 | 24.8 → 225.6 | 3.4 → 206.2 |
| Wandering load | 60 → 10 s | 45 → 270 | 37.4 → 51.8 | 20.3 → 30.9 | 0 → 0 |
| Fairness | 60 → 10 s | 15 → 90 | 46.6 → 127.9 | 20.5 → 58.5 | 13.4 → 51.6 |
| Faults | 60 → 10 s | 15 → 90 | 27.9 → 47.6 | 18.8 → 31.5 | 0 → 0 |
| All four at once | 60 → 10 s | 45 → 270 | 34.7 → 52.9 | 21.0 → 31.4 | 0 → 0 |
| A real day (Google 2011) | 60 → 10 s | 43 → 259 | 51.4 → 72.6 | 39.1 → 48.4 | 0 → 0 |
| Batch beside the service | 60 → 10 s | 25 → 150 | 12.7 → 10.9 | 2.1 → 1.6 | 0 → 0.4 |
| Steady (the control) | 60 → 10 s | 15 → 90 | 15.9 → 19.3 | 12.7 → 13.6 | 0 → 0 |

The loop. A raise lets the serving pods use more of the shared host. The host's busy share climbs to the guard's 75% top, and
the next pass hands every limit back. The host falls under 70%, and the next pass raises again. At 60 s a raise stood about
230 s before the guard took it back. At 10 s it stands two beats, about 20 s, so the service spends much of each half-minute at
the operator's 500m limit, and every switch is a write to a running pod. Where no other workload has room beside the service,
the guard never fires: no hand-back in wandering, faults, all four, the real day or steady. There the faster beat only shares
the machines' idle processor out again more often.

## 5. The controlled check already on record

The real pace runs on omni-v25 (`results/pace/PACE.md`, `docs/PACE_PREREGISTRATION.md` amendment 3) held the engine, the days,
the machines and the load the same and moved only the beat: the steady load, native and Omni-Compass at the factory 10 s and at
5, 15, 30, 75, 90 and 120 s, ten paired repetitions a run, three runs (class L). Against the 10 s beat, every slower beat read
no LOSS and five or six GAIN rows. At 75 s:

- the 95th percentile **GAIN by the mean of our three runs** (down 3.4%, 0.9% and 1.8%)
- the 99th percentile **GAIN by the mean** (down 5.9%, 5.4% and 6.2%)
- the mean **GAIN by the mean**
- the replicas held **GAIN in all three of our runs** (down 16.2%, 13.0% and 13.5%)

So the beat moves the service by itself, even on the steady load, where the guard never fires. The index above barely
moved there (+30.94% → +30.76%). Where the guard fires every two beats, the effect is many times larger.

## 6. What is established, and what is not

**Established from the files (class L):**
- every Omni arm's beat: 60 s behind omni-v21, 10 s behind omni-v25
- the six-fold rise in decisions
- the on/off cycle's counts and the governor's own reasons for it
- that the three workloads with another workload's room beside the service carry 68% of the fall
- that, with everything else held equal, a slower beat answers faster than 10 s on omni-v25's steady load

**Not established:**
- that 60 s on today's code would bring the two KEDA workloads and fairness back to their omni-v21 scores. No run has put those
  workloads at 60 s on omni-v22 to omni-v26.
- how much of the fall in wandering, all four, batch, the real day and fairness came from the runners themselves, since native
  moved there
- why faults slowed (native unchanged, no on/off cycle; its limit raises rose from 18.8 to 31.5 an arm)

## 7. Recovering the advantage without undoing a repair (no paid run)

None of the repairs made since omni-v21 depends on the beat: omni-v22's hand-back tried again, omni-v23's refused own scale
handed to native's autoscaler, and omni-v26's hold while another workload's pods wait. Taking the advantage back therefore
undoes none of them. Each candidate below is a complete fingerprinted engine on omni-v26, never files copied across versions
(the record of 9 November, `docs/audits/V25_V26_CHAMPION_SELECTION_2026-11-09.md`):

- **Candidate A:** omni-v26 with the on-site factory beat at 60 s, omni-v21's beat. The guard cycles as slowly as it did
  behind the +28.0% reading.
- **Candidate B:** omni-v26 at 10 s with the damped guard of the held omni-v27 candidate (branch `v27-convey-study`): a raise
  sized to the host's room under the band's cushion, and at the top half a raise handed back each pass, never all at once. Its
  model gate read no LOSS, and its replay of Koordinator's queue cut the cycle by 60.6%. Both are class S.

**The tests.** Two, both on GitHub's own machines, no cloud, no card, no rental, each preregistered before its runs read
(`docs/PACE_PREREGISTRATION.md`):
- **Amendment 7** (dispatched 07:09 UTC on 11 November; runs A 42977431782, B 42977442687, C 42977447533): the wandering load,
  native beside Omni-Compass at 10, 60 and 90 s in every repetition. The guard does not fire there (section 4), so this test reads
  what the beat does by itself on a moving load.
- **Amendment 8**: the three workloads with a neighbour, where the guard fires (KEDA's HTTP add-on, KEDA on the processor and the
  requests, fairness), native beside Omni-Compass at 10 s and at 60 s in every repetition, three runs each; the KEDA workflow takes
  an `arms` input for it. Candidate B (the damped guard) is tested the same way once it stands as a complete fingerprinted
  candidate. The model gate cannot decide this one: the corrected-model ladder that picked 10 s
on 2 November has no shared host whose busy share answers the conveyance, so it could not see this loop. The real runs are the
evidence here. The winner lands as the next version only by the standing rules, and the website carries no number from it until
its three-run tables exist.

The mechanism of section 4 is confirmed on the engine that stands: at 10 s the guard raises a serving pod's limit and hands it
back about every 20 seconds beside the neighbour's room, and at 60 s it all but stops. Nothing lands from this reading alone.
The amendment's rule stands as written: the on-site beat goes to the model gate only on no LOSS against 10 s, and one row reads
LOSS, so that row's cause is read first from the runs' own pod records, and the fairness runs are read before anything is
decided. A beat other than the factory's is a changed tune: these tables show the engine's range and are never presented as a
result of the factory tune, and no index or website number is read from them.
