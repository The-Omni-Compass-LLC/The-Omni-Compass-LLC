The founder's words of 16 November 2026: "My dear Watson, I don't give a damn ... 1 plus 1 make 2 and nobody can defend it ...
The math is what it is and nobody can do anything about it." This page fixes that voice for every document the company writes
for a reader outside it: the run-set reports (`tools/run_reports.py`), the manuals, the monograph's prose, the website's words.

## What the statement says

The line fuses two characters. Sherlock Holmes, addressing Watson, is the voice of the man who has already reasoned it through
and is waiting for the room to catch up (the famous "Elementary, my dear Watson" is a later coinage; Conan Doyle's Holmes says
"Elementary" and "my dear Watson" separately). Rhett Butler's "Frankly, my dear, I don't give a damn" (*Gone with the Wind*,
1939) is the voice of a man who no longer needs the other party's approval. Together: *I have done the reasoning, and your
approval is not part of it.*

The psychology is status by indifference. Whoever needs agreement argues; whoever does not, states. The statement claims
authority not by raising its voice but by withdrawing the need for consent.

Its one weakness is the whole point of this page. Indifference to opinion is strength only while it rests on something no one can
dispute. Aimed at the reader, it reads as contempt and loses the boardroom. Aimed at the evidence, it is unanswerable: a claim
that rests on arithmetic does not need anyone to like it. So the voice is indifferent to *opinion* and entirely bound to *proof*.
The math says it; the company only reads it out.

## The rules

1. **The number first.** The first sentence of every document is the result with its size: "Omni-Compass brought 500 of 500
   runs to a stable basin. The plant left alone brought 477."
2. **State; never argue.** No "we believe", "may", "might", "appears to", "hopefully", "we think". A sentence either stands on a
   record or is not written.
3. **No praise words.** No "revolutionary", "breakthrough", "amazing", "industry-leading". The numbers do the bragging.
4. **Every claim carries its receipt.** A file, a digest, a run on GitHub, and the command that repeats it. The challenge is
   the proof: "Check it. One command, any machine, the same digest."
5. **Limits are boundaries, not apologies.** "This is a model. It does not measure your cloud bill; the cloud tables do." Never
   "unfortunately", "only", "merely", "we regret".
6. **Losses in the same flat voice as gains.** A LOSS is printed beside every GAIN with its size and its cause. Showing the
   loss without flinching is what makes the gain impossible to dismiss.
7. **Never one word past the record.** An overclaim is the only opening anyone ever gets. The voice is unanswerable because every
   sentence survives a referee; the moment it claims more than the receipts carry, it is just loud. Class S stays class S, and
   "no outside party has reviewed or approved these results" stays on every result.
8. **Short sentences for the boardroom; exact terms for the engineer.** Same facts, same numbers, two registers. The engineer gets
   the equations and the intervals; the board gets three sentences and a table.
9. **No profanity in anything published.** The indifference is in the certainty, not in the words. A document that swears can be
   dismissed for its tone; a document that only states facts cannot be dismissed at all.

## Where it stands

- The run-set reports for the engineer, the CTO and the boardroom (`results/run_reports/latest/`, written by
  `tools/run_reports.py` on every 500-run set GitHub runs) open with the number, put the check-it-yourself command second, and
  close with where the evidence stops.
- Every other page is brought to it as it is next rewritten. The one key (`tools/readings.py`), the legal notice
  (`tools/legal.py`) and the honesty rules (`CLAUDE.md`, "Honesty") are not changed by it: the voice is how the record is read
  out, never what the record says.
