Run and scored by The Omni-Compass LLC's own code. No outside party has reviewed or approved these results. Preregistered means the rules were written down in our own repository before the runs; nothing was filed with any outside body.

Every measure of every test is a ratio oriented so that above 1 is a GAIN against the system's declared aim with Omni-Compass on top of native: work (more), speed (a lower response time), machines (fewer: a cut), energy (less: a cut). Below 1 is a LOSS for work and speed, and power given for machines and energy (the key: `docs/HOW_TO_READ_THE_RESULTS.md`). A test's index is the geometric mean of its scored ratios; a category is the geometric mean of its tests; the headline is the geometric mean of the real categories, each weighted the same. Scored as measured: work, speed and machines, the software end's own results, and energy where a meter read it (the card's own meter). Everywhere else (a declared power model's watts on kind, the host's processor seconds on the data stores: no meter on a GitHub runner) the energy is a model's estimate about the chip: it is shown beside every test and scored as native's, the same juice (the founder's words of 31 October: "more work ... for that same juice and for that same bill"), never dropped, because a geometric mean of fewer measures would weigh the rest more and read higher with nothing gained. Modelled muscles are shown beside it, never inside it. Every number is read from one engine's three-run tables (`results/live/V<n>_*.json`, the newest engine with every test's three runs whole, made by `tools/confirm_abc.py`, `tools/pgbench_abc.py`, `tools/kafka_abc.py`, `tools/redis_abc.py`, `tools/ycsb_abc.py` and `tools/sysbench_abc.py` from the three archived runs; `tools/omni_index.py`).

## The second reading, declared 2026-10-09: service alone, **96.5%, GAIN** (work and speed scored; machines and energy shown, not scored)

The founder asked what a cache is for. Redis is bought for speed and spends memory to deliver it; when this reading was declared, the index counted that memory at the same weight as the speed, so the Redis table of that day read −25% while its work and hit rate were a GAIN in all three of our runs (the table that stands now, after the cost fixes of 9 to 10 October, reads STEADY on every counted workload). The question is fair and the answer is a second reading, not a changed first one: the same tests, the same three-run rule, scored on the service the stack is bought for (work inside the line and the 95th percentile) with the resources it spent or saved shown beside it and not scored. It is computed for every test alike, never for one stack, and the preregistered reading above stays the headline. The two readings say different true things. Where the governor buys service with a resource (Redis's memory ceiling, Kafka's consumers) the service reading is higher and the resource reading says what it took; where it gives a resource back for the same service (the database pools and caches) the service reading is STEADY and the resource reading carries the cut, a GAIN. Kafka's speed ratio is a hundredfold (native's queue grew at nine tenths of its capacity by design), so the service reading leans on it; a reader who wants the number without that lean has the category table, where each category is one row. Neither reading hides a LOSS: less work or a slower response in all three of our runs counts below 1 in both.


Read: every column points the same way: a plus is a GAIN against the system's declared aims; a minus is a LOSS in the work and speed columns, and power given in the machines and energy columns (more of the resource put in). "Fewer machines by +4%" means Omni did the same work on 4% fewer machine-hours; "less energy by +3%" means 3% less energy for the same work; "faster by +95%" means native's slowest-5% response is 1.95 times Omni's (Omni answers about twice as fast); "more work by +45%" means 45% more work inside the response line. The energy figure on GitHub's Kubernetes is a declared model, not a meter; the database's, the messaging and the cache's energy columns are the host's CPU seconds (the compass's own CPU included; a rise in all three of our runs is power given and counts below 1); their machines columns are the resource held: connections open to the database, consumers running, the cache's memory ceiling, the storage engine's cache size, the buffer pool's size; Azure's machines are its own billed count.
