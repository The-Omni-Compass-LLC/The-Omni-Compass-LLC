Where every run moved the same way, clear of zero, the word reads in all three of our runs; otherwise by the mean of our three runs, with the count of runs that stood beyond the noise. The numbers are the runs' own; only the words were read again, and this rule replaces the one the text below describes.

MySQL exactly as Ubuntu ships it, its InnoDB buffer pool at MySQL's own default (128 MB), is native (`docs/MYSQL_PREREGISTRATION.md`, amendment 5); omni is the compass law on the pool size through the server's own console inside [128, 2,048] MB in chunks of 128 MB, holding the server's own mean statement latency at 40% of the 0.6 ms statement line (`docs/MYSQL_PREREGISTRATION.md`). sysbench's published OLTP workloads ask for rows from a set of tables that steps through the pool and past it, the same transactions in both arms. Each run holds three paired repetitions, each with the paired difference's 95% interval. A change of 0.5% or more, the mean of the three runs, is a change (the founder's order of 24 October 2026): every run the same way and clear of zero reads **GAIN** or **LOSS in all three of our runs**, otherwise **GAIN** or **LOSS by the mean of our three runs** with the count of runs beyond the noise; the word is judged by what the gauge is for and never by its sign (a resource spent that rose reads **power given**, never a loss). A mean under 0.5% reads **STEADY, under the floor**; runs that moved 0.5% or more in opposite directions read **the runs disagree**. Errors: any rise in any run reads **LOSS in at least one of our runs**. Memory held is the resource; the host's CPU-seconds, the compass's own cost included, are measured on GitHub's shared runner, and no energy is claimed beyond them. The tuning workload is shown and not counted. Every row is shown, LOSS included.
