Seeds 1 to 8, 1.0 day each where a day is run; the standing engine omni-v10, the candidate omni-v11. Evidence class S, our own models: a reading here says whether a change shows promise before a real run, never what a benchmark found. Each candidate reading is paired by seed with the standing engine's (the mean change, its 95% interval over the seeds, the word by the gauge's aim: tools/readings.py).

omni-v11 adds one guard to the Kubernetes governor: a reading with no machine in service is held, never read as an overload. This is the first gate run with each arm proven to import its own checkout's engine (from omni-v9's gate on, both arms had run the candidate's; see the corrections on `V9_VS_V8.md` and `V10_VS_V9.md`), and with the blink model: the cluster model with every machine out of service in the governor's reading for two minutes every six hours, the failure the omni-v10 CruiseKube test met. In it omni-v10's decision failed 64 times over the eight seeds (a division by zero, each writing nothing) and omni-v11 held 64 times with no error; every gauge and every other count is identical, because a failed decision and a held one both write nothing. Against native the standing and candidate engines read the same, and four rows read LOSS in both: the pod start wait under conveyance (+9.0% mean, +10.0% at the 95th percentile), the pending-pod minutes and the response time at a decision every 15 s (+111.7%, +0.33%; no run decides that often), and the alert minutes in the blink model (+2.35%). They are the next version's work, in this gate first.
