Seeds 1 to 8, 1.0 day each where a day is run; the standing engine omni-v12 (main, digest d38b58fbe28939b8), the candidate omni-v13 candidate (branch v13-candidate). Evidence class S, our own models: a reading here says whether a change shows promise before a real run, never what a benchmark found. Each candidate reading is paired by seed with the standing engine's (the mean change, its 95% interval over the seeds, the word by the gauge's aim: tools/readings.py).

The candidate is omni-v12 with one fault fixed in the Kubernetes governor's conveyance (omni_controller/muscles.py): on a shared host at its top (busy at or over 75%), while another workload there has room of its own (a pod whose limit is above its request), every limit given goes back to the operator's and is conveyed again only under 70%; and every other pod on a machine is counted by its own limit, where omni-v12 counted requests (docs/OMNI_V13.md). The fault was found on KubeRay's rig with the organism alone (docs/BATCH_QUEUE_PREREGISTRATION.md, amendment 11). This gate adds a model, convey_batch: KubeRay's batch muscle beside the serving one on the batch rig's host, the kind nodes on 3 of its 4 cores and the governor alone on the fourth, the stream of 24 jobs (one every 30 s, ten pods each, five at a time, 500m requested and a 1-core limit), each pod's work set so the native queue finishes where the saturated runners' did (834 s), the processor shared between cgroups by the weights of their requests, the pods' measured start lag (2.66 s, rounded up to 3) and the governor's own reading of the host (its busy share over the time since its previous reading, none at the first). The batch pods' loss of work while a serving pod's quota is lifted above its operator's on a host at its top is the saturated KubeRay repetitions' own (hashing 7.64% slower, so 0.071 of the work); the model takes it from those files and does not derive it, so this model shows whether the hand-back fires where and when it should, and what it costs or saves elsewhere, not the size of the real effect. On this 3-core host both engines serve 2% to 3% fewer requests than native, with no batch at all as well, while the real KubeRay runs served 4% more after the queue: the model's web model is out of its regime there (it was set from the 4-core KEDA run), and that reading is carried on the next version's list (docs/CHANGE_CONTROL.md) rather than read as a result.
